The catch: a paused machine still holds its quota

All fifteen jobs share 20 machines and 320 vCPUs. A stopped machine uses no electricity but keeps its slot, so pausing one job through a dirty night can leave the next job with nowhere to run.

Our first version paused each job on its own. Offline it delivered only 9 to 12 of the 15 jobs on time. Missing a single deadline costs the 5,000-point completion bonus, more than a good run loses to carbon in total.

So the planner books every job on one shared calendar of quota, hour by hour, and pauses only where the calendar has room.

How the planner decides, every simulated hour

  1. Price every hour

    For each job and legal zone: machine power × PUE × forecast grams per kWh. Beyond the 24-hour forecast it uses a blend of the forecast's pattern and mean, plus a 10% risk premium, so known clean hours win over guesses.

  2. Give each job a block

    A block is a window of 1 to 2 times the job's run time. The job runs in the cleanest hours inside it and is stopped in the rest.

  3. Book quota for the whole block

    vCPUs, GPU cards and machines are reserved for every hour of the block, because stopped machines keep them. Running jobs are booked first, then the least flexible.

  4. Check residency twice

    A zone outside a job's allowed countries is never offered, and every create is checked again right before the API call.

  5. Act and explain

    Delete finished machines, stop, start, create. Every decision is logged with its reason, which feeds our Control Room.

    On the live platform

    Five practice runs, four of them inside our Docker image at 720× speed, the judges' setup. All stayed qualified. Runs 1, 2 and 4 delivered all 15 jobs on time; the first topped the preliminary leaderboard on Friday. Run 3 left one parallel job without room until its deadline. Now a job that finds no room, or only a late slot, is planned first, and in run 4 that job finished 22 hours early. In run 5 the platform took 65 seconds to answer one status request, about 13 simulated hours, and a paused job that was nearly done resumed too late. Now a job with 12 or fewer hours of work, or of spare time, left runs straight to the end. Real-run carbon and scores stay on the platform's dashboard, because the rules forbid publishing a scenario's real values; the run figures here come from our own decision logs.

    How the score works

    We checked this formula against the platform's own per-job breakdown: each job earns up to its share of 20,000 points, divided by 1 + 0.75 × its carbon cost + 0.25 × the run's carbon cost, both measured against 200 g/kWh. All 15 on time adds 5,000. Every gram counts twice: once for its job and once for the whole run.

    Rules it never breaks

    Data residency

    One attempt in a wrong country disqualifies the run, even if the API rejects it. Allowed countries are resolved from each job's rules (EU excludes Switzerland, the forbid list always wins) and checked in the planner and again before every create.

    Forecasts only

    The planner's only electricity input is the run's own forecast. No actuals, no other runs, no stored series. The offline grids used on this page are synthetic.

    Never lose work

    Machines are deleted only after the final output is delivered. After a crash the controller restarts and adopts the machines that are already running.

    Stay inside the run

    Every request is checked against the run's own URL prefix and paced under the platform's rate limits. Credentials come from the environment and never touch the repository.
